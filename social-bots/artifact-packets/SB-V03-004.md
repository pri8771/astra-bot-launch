## Goal

Exactly one worker may own a task/runtime fence during normal acquisition, stale takeover, and the entire active work unit.

## Required properties

- stale read + takeover must be conditionally atomic/fenced;
- two contenders cannot both believe they acquired ownership;
- losing contender receives an explicit non-owner result;
- previous stale owner's uncertain external effects are reconciled before new eligible effects;
- fencing remains correct across restart;
- receipts identify lease/fence generation or equivalent ownership token;
- lease validity covers the entire work unit, not only acquisition;
- an old owner that loses/expires its fence cannot commit state, content, experiments, receipts that imply success, or external-effect eligibility afterward.

## Lead finding at head 2cab7219

The new `fcntl.flock` critical section fixes the simultaneous stale-takeover acquisition race on one POSIX host, but it does not yet fence a worker whose lease expires while `decision.run_cycle()` is still executing.

`worker.run_one_unit()` renews immediately before `decision.run_cycle()`. The decision cycle can then write state/content/experiment data without another ownership check. If runtime exceeds the TTL, a contender can acquire the stale lease while the original process is still running; the old process can continue and commit after fence loss. A failed final `release()` is too late to prevent that commit.

Do not repair this merely by increasing the TTL. Provide a real active-cycle lease/fencing solution and an adversarial regression.
