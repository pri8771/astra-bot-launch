## Goal

Exactly one worker may own a task/runtime fence during normal acquisition, stale takeover, the entire active work unit, and durable completion evidence that implies successful ownership.

## Required properties

- stale read + takeover must be conditionally atomic/fenced;
- two contenders cannot both believe they acquired ownership;
- losing contender receives an explicit non-owner result;
- previous stale owner's uncertain external effects are reconciled before new eligible effects;
- fencing remains correct across restart;
- receipts identify lease/fence generation or equivalent ownership token;
- lease validity covers the entire work unit and success completion evidence, not only acquisition;
- an old owner that loses/expires its fence cannot commit state, content, experiments, receipts that imply success, or external-effect eligibility afterward.

## Resolved earlier findings

The current Core branch materially repairs the prior acquisition and migration problems:
- POSIX `flock` serializes fresh acquisition/stale takeover on one local host;
- generation fencing prevents an obsolete owner from committing after takeover;
- `PersonaState.load()` stages legacy migration in memory without durable writes;
- migration/persona/runtime/decision/content/experiment/action writes in the decision cycle flow through the final ownership-fenced commit.

These are positive verified source changes and must be preserved.

## LEAD-024 remaining defect — post-cycle success receipt

`runtime/worker.py::run_one_unit()` calls the ownership-fenced `decision.run_cycle(..., fence=fence)`, then writes the `finish` receipt after that call returns and outside the fence.

Adversarial scenario:
1. owner A completes the decision cycle's fenced durable commit;
2. A stalls before writing its finish receipt;
3. A's lease TTL expires;
4. owner B takes over and increments the fence generation;
5. A resumes and currently can write a finish receipt with `candidate_succeeded=true` / `verified=true` even though A no longer owns the task.

This violates the artifact's success-evidence fencing contract. A failed final `release()` does not undo a misleading finish receipt.

## Required repair after LEAD-024

1. Fence or atomically ownership-check every durable post-cycle completion record that can imply successful ownership/execution, including the success/finish receipt.
2. After takeover, the obsolete owner must not emit a success/finish receipt.
3. Truthful failure/fenced-out evidence may remain, but cannot be ambiguous with successful completion.
4. Add an adversarial test that pauses after the cycle commit, forces TTL expiry + generation takeover, resumes the old owner, and proves no success-implying finish receipt is written.
5. Preserve the side-effect-free migration path and all existing fenced cycle writes.
6. Do not repair by only extending the TTL.

## Acceptance

- concurrent fresh acquisition with multiple contenders yields exactly one owner;
- concurrent stale takeover with multiple contenders yields exactly one owner;
- force the active worker's lease to expire while it is executing, then allow takeover: the old owner cannot commit cycle state/effects afterward;
- force takeover after the cycle commit but before completion receipt: the old owner cannot emit a success/finish receipt afterward;
- ownership/fence generation is inspectable after the race;
- no duplicate state/effect eligibility or misleading duplicate success evidence;
- reconciliation happens before the new owner performs eligible work;
- migration/load path remains side-effect free until fenced commit;
- all prior lease/fencing tests pass;
- host/filesystem scope is explicitly documented; native-Windows/cross-host claims require separate proof rather than assumption.
